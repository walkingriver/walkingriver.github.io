You aren't imagining it.

You have sprints, standups, story points, and a Jira board. Someone on your team is probably called a Scrum Master. There is a planning ceremony, a retrospective, maybe even a definition of done posted somewhere.

<!--more-->

You also have a QA phase at the end of every sprint. Your roadmap was handed to you in January, assembled from last October's capital planning process. The delivery dates were decided before the team estimated anything. When someone asks how many days a five-point story takes, the answer has somehow become official policy.

This is not a failure of Agile. It is not a failure of your team. It is the most common configuration in enterprise software development, and it has a name: **Agilefall**.

Agilefall is what happens when an organization adopts the ceremonies of agile methodology while keeping the structural assumptions of waterfall: fixed scope, fixed dates, capital budgets approved before discovery, and a QA phase that sits at the end rather than throughout. The sprints are real. The iteration is not.

I have seen this pattern across industries, company sizes, and technology stacks. The sprint planning argument that frustrated you last month ran on almost the same script at companies with nothing else in common. The retro action items that never change, the velocity used as a productivity target, the manager who needs exact dates for work nobody has estimated yet — these are structural symptoms, not individual failures.

Recognizing the hybrid you are actually in is more useful than trying to implement textbook Scrum in an organization that will not run that way. The question is not "how do we fix our Agile?" The question is "how do we ship software well inside the environment we actually have?"

That reframe changes which tactics make sense.

If you have been in sprints that felt more like marathons, planning sessions that felt like theater, and retros that covered the same ground every two weeks — you are not imagining the dysfunction. You are in the most populated zip code in enterprise software.
